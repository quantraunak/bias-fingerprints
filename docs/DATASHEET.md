# Datasheet

Following Gebru et al., *Datasheets for Datasets*. Numbers are measured as of
2026-09-08. Where a limitation is known, it is stated here rather than left for
a user to discover.

## Motivation

**Why was it created?** Economic links between firms — who supplies whom, who
sells to whom — are disclosed in the prose of SEC 10-K filings but are not
present in any free structured source. Commercial supply-chain graphs exist
(FactSet Revere, Bloomberg SPLC) but are proprietary, and to our knowledge none
is published point-in-time, meaning edges keyed to the date the market could
first have seen them. It is released alongside a construction protocol —
`EXTRACTION.md` for the extractor choice, `HYPOTHESIS.md` for the pre-registered
coverage gate — so that the graph and the method for building another one ship
together.

**Who created it?** One person, as independent research. No funding, no API
spend, all computation on one laptop.

## Composition

Three artefacts, of decreasing maturity.

**1. Extraction benchmark (`gold_links.json`, `gold_exclusions.md`).**
10 filings, 55 annotated links, **5 filings annotated empty on purpose**. The
empty filings are rich-looking: First Solar names eleven companies, all inside
executive biographies; Qualcomm names six, all acquisitions, transaction
counterparties or regulators. Every excluded name is listed with the rule that
excluded it, so the negatives are auditable rather than implicit. Two filings
name ten or more counterparties.

**2. Extraction frontier (`extraction_table.md`).** Six model configurations
scored on that benchmark, with errors decomposed into false positives on empty
filings, relation-direction errors, and recall split by list length.

**3. Link graph (`link_claims.csv.gz`, `link_edges.csv.gz`).** 933 validated
claims from 163 filings across 38 issuers, filing dates 2010-02 to 2025-11. 400
claims (42.9%) resolve to a listed ticker; 264 become edges. 46% of filings
yield no claim.

**What does an instance represent?** A claim is one asserted relationship
between the filing company and a named counterparty, carrying the verbatim
sentence it came from. An edge is a resolved claim with a validity interval.

**Is anything missing?** Yes, and systematically:

- **The graph is a pilot, not a corpus.** 163 of a planned 2,364 filings, and 38
  of 160 issuers. Coverage is not uniform across issuers or years.
- **Historical company names do not resolve.** Names are matched against
  EDGAR's *current* registry, so Raytheon, Navistar, Chrysler and
  Alcatel-Lucent resolve to nothing. Coverage is biased against renamed and
  acquired firms — a survivorship-flavoured gap.
- **Only what firms chose to disclose in prose.** Disclosure is correlated with
  materiality thresholds under ASC 280, which compels disclosure above 10% of
  revenue but does not compel naming. This is a selected sample of the real
  link structure, and the selection is not random.
- **Full filing text is not included.** Accession numbers are; the text is
  re-fetchable from EDGAR.

**Confidential or offensive content?** No. All source material is public
corporate disclosure.

## Collection process

Filings were downloaded from SEC EDGAR at the rate limit the SEC requests, under
the original submission (not later amendments folded in). Each document is
stored under its EDGAR filing date. Text was extracted with BeautifulSoup, with
long tables dropped and short ones kept — many filers lay section headings out
in one-row tables.

Passages were selected by `src/graph/passages.py`, which scores paragraphs on
named entities near relationship language rather than on keyword density. **The
benchmark is defined over selected passages, not over whole filings**: the
extractor only ever sees the selection, so a link in a dropped section is a
selector failure, not an extraction failure, and conflating them would make the
extraction numbers uninterpretable.

Benchmark filings were drawn by `scripts/sample_gold.py`, stratified across
issuers and filing years with a fixed seed, keeping filings with no resolvable
candidate in proportion. A benchmark made only of rich filings would overstate
precision for every model.

## Preprocessing, cleaning, labeling

Claims are generated by a local LLM (Qwen 3 32B, Q4_K_M, temperature 0,
schema-constrained decoding) and then **validated**: the evidence quote must be
a literal substring of the passages the model was shown. Claims failing that
check are discarded and *counted*, so hallucination is a reported rate rather
than an assumption.

Name resolution is tiered — exact, token-set, then subset gated on registry
document frequency of the shared tokens. Edit distance is deliberately not used:
"Delta Air Lines" and "Delta Apparel" are close in edit distance and unrelated.

Annotation rules for four recurring cases (government buyers, part-owned joint
ventures, relationships named only inside executive biographies, historical
agreements cited for an unrelated purpose) are recorded in `SPECIFICATIONS.md`
and applied uniformly. Annotations carry the evidence span and a
`related_party` tag, so a different ruling is a re-filter rather than a
re-annotation.

**Labeling limitation, and it is the significant one: a single annotator, with
no inter-annotator agreement measured.** The rules are written down and the
exclusions auditable, which makes the sample reproducible, but not
independently validated. `scripts/annotator_agreement.py` computes agreement
when a second annotation exists; none does yet.

## Uses

**Used for so far:** measuring extraction quality across six model
configurations, and a coverage projection for a return study that was stopped
before any forward return was regressed.

**Appropriate uses.** Evaluating relation extraction from financial filings —
this is the intended use, and the auditable negatives are what make it
worthwhile. Prototyping link-graph construction. Studying the disclosure
behaviour itself.

**Inappropriate uses, explicitly:**

- **Not a substitute for a commercial supply-chain graph.** Half of filings
  disclose nothing and coverage is a third of the intended issuer set.
- **Not a complete link structure**, and its incompleteness is correlated with
  firm characteristics, so treating absence of an edge as absence of a
  relationship will bias any study that does.
- **Not suitable for point-in-time work sensitive to renaming** until historical
  names resolve.
- **Not a trading signal.** No return has been regressed on any graph quantity.
- **The `confidence` column must not be used as a weight.** It is degenerate:
  the model returned "high" for 1,051 of 1,060 claims in one run and all 74 in
  another. It is retained for provenance only.
- **Benchmark results at n=10 do not resolve small differences.** Order-of-magnitude
  gaps (2 vs 27 false positives on empty filings) are real; two points of F1
  between adjacent configurations are not.

## Distribution

Derived tables and benchmark annotations under **CC BY 4.0**. Code under MIT.
Evidence quotes are quoted from registrant filings and are not licensed by this
project — see `RELEASE.md` for why the "SEC filings are public domain"
shorthand is not relied on. Prices and anything derived from prices are **not**
distributed; users fetch their own.

Model provenance matters for licensing: the shipped claims are Qwen 3 (Apache
2.0). Llama 3 outputs exist in the repository as model-comparison evidence and
carry the Meta Llama 3 Community License, including its attribution requirement
and restriction on using outputs to train other models.

## Maintenance

Maintained by the author on a best-effort basis. The benchmark is versioned
(v1 archived as `gold_links_v1.json`, v2 current); scoring any new extractor is
`scripts/benchmark_table.py`, which reads from cache and needs no GPU. Errata
and rule changes are appended to `SPECIFICATIONS.md` rather than edited in
place, so the record of what was decided when is preserved.
