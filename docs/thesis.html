You asked whether this can beat 18 percent.
It probably can't — and that's the company.

A twenty-year run at 18% CAGR is roughly eight points a year ahead of the S&P over the same span. It is real, it is rare, and it was almost certainly produced by concentration rather than by an engine. The useful product is not a promise to repeat it. It is the machine that tells a family exactly what it can engineer, at what confidence, and what the target actually costs.

To reach 18% in expectation, the model requires roughly 81% of net worth in a single name expected to beat the market by 8% a year — lifting portfolio volatility to 36% and expected worst drawdown to −87%.

That is a concentration decision, not a diversification strategy. It is also, almost certainly, how the original track record was built. Saying this plainly is more valuable to a family than any pitch deck, and no incumbent will say it.

The gap

Everyone optimizes a sleeve. Nobody optimizes the balance sheet.

The market splits cleanly into three groups, and the seam between them is the opportunity.

  • Data without decisions. Addepar aggregates and reports. It will show a family its concentration; it will not tell them what to do about it.
  • Decisions without depth. Arta Finance and Compound Planning deliver a genuine digital family office — Compound grew past $4B this year — but the quantitative layer underneath is thin. They are distribution and service businesses.
  • Depth without context. AQR, Quantinno and Aperio run serious engines on one sleeve, blind to the estate, the concentrated position, the wrappers, and the spending.

A family with $28M in six accounts, a founder position at 30% of net worth, an estate heading through the exemption, and $9.7M of dated goals does not have a sleeve problem. Every one of those interacts: the concentration unwind produces the gains that make harvesting valuable; the estate structure determines whether to gift or hold for step-up; the wrapper mix determines where the long/short can live at all.

The wedge: after-tax, whole-balance-sheet optimization. Almost every incumbent optimizes pre-tax and per-account. Tax and structure are where the reliable points are — and they are exactly the points that only appear when you look at the household as one object.

What's underneath

Three engines, each already built once

The unusual thing here is that the three hardest components map onto work already done rather than onto a roadmap.

Public alpha

Tax-aware long/short

Cross-sectional gradient-boosted ranking into a convex optimizer under dollar- and beta-neutral constraints. The extension: add lot-level cost basis as a state variable so the optimizer trades off expected return against realized gains.

From: r1000-ls-strategy. LightGBM ranker on 10 factors, purged walk-forward CV with 21-day embargo, Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage, CVXPY/CLARABEL, vol targeting and a drawdown governor. Backtest Sharpe 1.46, CI [0.90, 2.07].
Private selection

Manager & deal scoring

Manager dispersion, not the illiquidity premium, decides whether private markets pay. A scoring model over funds, GPs and co-invests is the only defensible way to underwrite that at this asset level.

From: Innovius Capital. CatBoost plus LLM-as-a-judge scoring, which moved deal-advancement accuracy from ~30% to ~80% — structurally the same problem as fund selection.
Research synthesis

Explanation layer

Every recommendation has to survive a family member asking "why". Retrieval over filings, fund documents and tax authority, with citations, is what converts a model output into something a principal will sign.

From: QIAGEN. GraphRAG embedding-based reasoning over pathway data and a fine-tuned retrieval system that cut research time 70%.

That is a genuine founder-market fit story: a quant engine, a selection model, and a retrieval layer, each shipped in a different domain, converging on one product.

Three constraints worth designing around rather than discovering later. Backtested performance is heavily restricted in marketing under the Marketing Rule — which is precisely why a probability distribution is a better centerpiece than a return claim. Performance fees require qualified clients. And anything personalized and compensated needs registration, so the product surface and the regulatory posture have to be designed together.

Go to market

Sell to the person with a dated, quantified problem

Broad wealth management is a referral business and nearly impossible to enter cold. The concentrated-position holder is not: someone post-IPO, post-acquisition, or post-secondary has a problem with a number attached and a deadline. The engine answers it in one sitting — required alpha to justify holding, ranked strategies, tax cost of each.

That is a wedge with three properties worth having: the prospect self-identifies through a public liquidity event; the analysis is genuinely hard to do by hand; and solving it deposits the entire balance sheet — with lot-level basis history — onto the platform. The tax alpha then compounds into switching cost that is literal rather than notional: unwinding creates a taxable event, and the basis history is not portable.

Los Altos and the USC network sit on top of the largest concentration of exactly this profile in the country.

Risks

What would kill this

  • Distribution, not technology. The engine is the easy part. Compound raised venture money and merged to reach $4B; this is a business where trust compounds slower than code.
  • The incumbents move down-market. BlackRock and Morgan Stanley already own Aperio and Parametric. If either opens direct access at scale, the sleeve commoditizes — which is the argument for owning the household layer rather than the sleeve.
  • Regulatory drag is real. Registration, custody and the Marketing Rule are months of work and a permanent cost, not a formality.
  • The tax alpha is a deferral, not a gift. Harvesting drives terminal embedded gains toward 100%. Honest modeling has to carry that liability, and clients must be told.
  • A single-model alpha sleeve is fragile. One signal set, one researcher, one codebase, on a survivorship-biased backtest universe. It needs live track record before it is sized above single digits.
