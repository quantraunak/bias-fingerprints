\begin{abstract}
\noindent
A table of factor $t$-statistics is usually all an outside reader gets. The allocator
reading a manager's factor deck, the referee reading a submitted backtest, the desk
pricing a vendor signal: none of them can run the pipeline that produced the numbers,
and the literature on look-ahead and survivorship bias is written for someone who can.

We propose \emph{bias fingerprinting}, a framework that turns a controlled research
pipeline into a diagnostic instrument for uncontrolled ones. Each data-handling defect is
represented as the vector of shifts it induces across a fixed factor cross-section; that
vector is measured once on a pipeline the analyst owns, then used as a known quantity in
a hypothesis test on a reported table. The framework has four parts: a generator that
re-runs one study under alternative data conventions, a measured signature per defect, an
inference model that separates signature from unknown true performance, and a
pre-specified validation protocol.

We instantiate it on a twenty-two factor US equity study and calibrate two signatures.
Joining fundamentals on fiscal period end rather than filing date inflates mean
information coefficient by 59\% among the eleven factors that read a filed figure and
manufactures four $t$-statistics above $2.0$, while leaving the eleven price-only factors
unchanged to machine precision. Conditioning the universe on current index membership
does not inflate at all: mean IC \emph{falls} by $0.0043$ while \fac{amihud\_illiquidity}
flips from $t = -1.11$ to $+2.80$ and the low-volatility anomaly inverts. The two
signatures correlate at $-0.088$ and disagree in sign on value factors, which is what
makes the diagnostic feasible.

We then run the protocol. Both signatures are stable across disjoint subsamples and
survive bootstrapping, but separability holds for only one of them. Against a
$\tau$ resembling a real factor table, universe conditioning is recovered at 100\%
accuracy while period-end dating is recovered at 52\% and labels 97\% of clean
tables defective. The reason is geometric and measurable in advance: after removing
family means the dating signature is smaller than the within-family variation of
true performance and correlates $+0.543$ with it, so a clean table already supplies
most of the defect's shape. This yields a condition a signature must satisfy before
it can be used diagnostically, checkable without collecting a single reported table.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Factor research is consumed as a table. An allocator sees mean IC, an information ratio
and a $t$-statistic for each of a manager's signals. A referee sees the same for a
submitted paper. A desk evaluating a vendor sees less. In none of these cases is the
pipeline available, and a Sharpe ratio carries no information about which construction
choice produced it.

The literature on data-handling bias is prescriptive and self-directed. Banz and Breen
\cite{banz1986} established that look-ahead inflates backtested returns; survivorship is
textbook. Both are taught as things to avoid in one's own code: date the fundamentals
correctly, build the universe point-in-time, shift a feature forward and check that the
result degrades. All of it assumes the reader controls the pipeline. Nothing in it helps
the reader who controls only the table.

This paper proposes a framework for that reader. The idea is that a data-handling defect
is not a scalar amount of inflation but a \emph{direction} in factor space, and that the
direction is a property of the defect rather than of the study it damages. A defect that
corrupts filed fundamentals cannot move a factor built from prices alone. A defect that
corrupts the universe moves liquidity and volatility factors hardest, because it selects
on size. Different defects therefore leave different patterns, and a pattern is something
a reader can see in a table.

\paragraph{The framework.} Bias fingerprinting has four components, set out in
Section~\ref{sec:framework}. A \emph{generator} re-runs one factor study under
alternative data conventions with everything else held fixed, so the difference between
two arms is attributable to the convention. A \emph{signature} $\delta_b$ is the vector of
$t$-statistic shifts a defect $b$ induces across the factor cross-section, measured on
that generator. An \emph{inference model} treats an observed table as true performance
plus a signature plus noise, and recovers the signature using exclusion restrictions and
within-family contrasts rather than a fitted classifier. A \emph{validation protocol}
states, in advance, the four measurements that have to succeed before the diagnostic may
be applied to a real table, and the order in which they are made.

The framework is not specific to the two defects calibrated here, or to equities. It
applies wherever a research pipeline can be re-run under a defective convention, which is
to say wherever the analyst owns a correct pipeline. That is what makes the labelled data
for validation free: biased panels are generated rather than collected, so the diagnostic
can be tested at scale with no annotation and no external dataset.

\paragraph{Calibration.} We instantiate the framework on a twenty-two factor
cross-sectional US equity study and calibrate two signatures. Joining fundamentals on
fiscal period end inflates mean IC by 59\% of its true value among the eleven factors that
read a filed figure, and turns four insignificant factors into $t$-statistics above 2.0.
Its eleven price-only factors do not move at all, with maximum absolute drift
$\num{0.0e+00}$: exact equality in floating point, which is the strongest form an
exclusion restriction can take. Conditioning the universe on current index membership
behaves differently in every respect. Mean IC falls by $0.00432$ and only 8 of 22 factors
improve, but \fac{amihud\_illiquidity} moves from $t = -1.11$ to $+2.80$,
\fac{turnover\_1m} becomes significant with the wrong sign, and the low-volatility anomaly
inverts. The channel is not the survival filter usually named: the biased panel wrongly
\emph{includes} 203 names a day that had not yet been admitted to the index, against 88 it
wrongly excludes, and the included cohort is a third the size and a third the liquidity of
a genuine constituent.

Across the twenty-two factors the two signature vectors correlate at $-0.088$, and they
disagree in sign on value: dating inflates \fac{earnings\_yield} and \fac{roe} by $+0.98$
and $+1.07$ $t$-units while universe conditioning deflates them by $-1.28$ and $-1.24$. A
table cannot be explained by both. Together with the exclusion restriction, that
separation is the empirical content the diagnostic rests on.

\paragraph{Contribution.} Three things are new. The framework itself, which reverses the
direction of the standard treatment of data-handling bias, from prescription for the
producer to diagnosis for the consumer. The two calibrated signatures, measured with
identical prices, universe, tradability screen and forward returns, changing one
convention at a time. And the characterisation of the universe channel as a
forward-looking growth filter rather than a survival filter, which explains why it
relocates measured performance instead of inflating it.

The framework is specified and calibrated; it is not yet validated as a test. Gates one
through three of Section~\ref{sec:protocol} have not been run, and we treat that ordering
as part of the method rather than as an omission: a diagnostic that reports a label
without a calibrated reference distribution is worse than no diagnostic.
Section~\ref{sec:framework} sets out the framework, Section~\ref{sec:data} the generator
it is instantiated on, Sections~\ref{sec:dating} and~\ref{sec:surv} the two signatures,
Section~\ref{sec:fingerprints} their separability, and Section~\ref{sec:protocol} the
validation protocol and the conditions under which the diagnostic transfers.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Three literatures bear on this, and none of them answers the question above.

\paragraph{The errors themselves are old and well characterised.} Banz and Breen
\cite{banz1986} established that look-ahead inflates measured returns in accounting-based
tests. Survivorship bias in performance evaluation was quantified by Brown, Goetzmann,
Ibbotson and Ross \cite{brown1992} and by Elton, Gruber and Blake \cite{elton1996}, and
Shumway \cite{shumway1997} and Shumway and Warther \cite{shumway1999} showed that even CRSP
needs care, because missing delisting returns bias the small-cap end of the cross-section.
None of that work is superseded here. What it does not do is characterise the \emph{shape}
of each distortion across a factor cross-section, which is the object this paper measures.

\paragraph{Work on false discovery in the factor zoo concerns multiplicity, not
provenance.} Lo and MacKinlay \cite{lo1990} formalised data-snooping bias. Harvey, Liu and
Zhu \cite{harvey2016} argue that the accumulated search over published factors demands a
$t$-statistic threshold near $3.0$. Bailey and L\'opez de Prado \cite{bailey2014deflated}
deflate the Sharpe ratio for selection and non-normality, and Bailey et al.
\cite{bailey2014pseudo} show how fast backtest overfitting manufactures apparent skill.
McLean and Pontiff \cite{mclean2016} document post-publication decay consistent with a
substantial share of published anomalies being artifacts.

That literature asks whether a result survives the number of hypotheses tested. It is silent
on whether a result is an artifact of how the panel was \emph{built}, and the two leave
different traces. Multiplicity inflates broadly and without regard to how a factor is
constructed; Section~\ref{sec:surv} exhibits a construction defect that \emph{lowers} mean IC
while manufacturing significance in one specific corner of the cross-section. No
multiple-testing correction would flag it, because nothing about the average looks improved.

\paragraph{The machine-learning literature names the mechanism but assumes access.} Kaufman,
Rosset and Perlich \cite{kaufman2012} give the canonical formulation of leakage and propose
detection through legitimacy checks on the training data. Kapoor and Narayanan
\cite{kapoor2023} document leakage across a wide range of published ML-based science and
argue it constitutes a reproducibility crisis rather than a set of isolated mistakes. Both
address a researcher holding the pipeline. The device used in Section~\ref{sec:dating} to
establish the exclusion restriction is metamorphic testing \cite{chen1998metamorphic}:
identify a transformation of the input under which the output must be invariant, apply it,
and treat any change as a defect. The idea is standard in software testing and, as far as we
have found, has not been applied to the construction of an asset-pricing panel.

\paragraph{Reproducibility infrastructure.} Chen and Zimmermann \cite{chen2022} release
open-source replications of a large set of published cross-sectional predictors, and
Novy-Marx and Velikov \cite{novymarx2016} price anomalies net of realistic trading costs.
Both establish that construction choices dominate reported performance. Neither treats the
construction choice as a quantity to be \emph{inferred} from a reported table.

\paragraph{Where this sits.} The forward direction --- what a given defect does to a given
study --- is a measurement in the tradition of the first group, made with the controlled
one-arm-at-a-time design the fourth group argues for. The inverse direction is what is new,
and Section~\ref{sec:protocol} is explicit that it is proposed rather than established.

\section{The framework}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{The generator}

Fix a factor registry, an evaluation window, a forward-return definition and a
walk-forward split. A generator is a pipeline that produces the resulting table of factor
$t$-statistics under any of a set of data conventions, sharing every component that the
convention does not touch. Prices, tradability screen, sector neutralisation,
rank-normalisation and evaluation code are common across arms; the arm changes one
construction decision.

The generator is what supplies the counterfactual. Without it, the effect of a defect can
only be argued; with it, the effect is a difference between two runs of the same code.
The requirement it imposes is real: the analyst must own a pipeline that is correct on the
dimension being studied, because the correct arm is the baseline everything is measured
against.

\subsection{The signature}

Let $t^{\text{pit}}$ be the vector of factor $t$-statistics from the correct arm and
$t^{b}$ the vector from the arm containing defect $b$. The signature is the difference,
\begin{equation}
\delta_b = t^{b} - t^{\text{pit}},
\label{eq:signature}
\end{equation}
one component per factor, with $\delta_{\text{none}} = 0$ by construction. A signature is
useful to the extent it has structure: components that are exactly zero, components that
are large, and a direction that other defects do not share. Sections~\ref{sec:dating}
and~\ref{sec:surv} measure $\delta_b$ for two defects and Section~\ref{sec:fingerprints}
reports the geometry of the pair.

\subsection{The inference problem}

An observed table from an unknown pipeline is a vector $t$ of factor $t$-statistics. Model
it as
\begin{equation}
t = \tau + \delta_b + \varepsilon,
\label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
where $\tau$ is the study's true factor performance, $\delta_b$ is the measured signature
of whichever defect the study contains, and $\varepsilon$ is sampling noise. The
diagnostic problem is to infer $b$.

Since $\tau$ is unknown and twenty-two dimensional, $b$ is not identified without further
structure. The framework uses three sources of it, in increasing order of assumption:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Exclusion.} Some defects cannot move some factors, for reasons of
construction rather than magnitude. Dating bias cannot move a factor that never reads a
filed figure. If a study's strongest results sit in the excluded block, that defect is
ruled out whatever $\tau$ is. This requires no assumption on $\tau$ at all.
\item \textbf{Within-family contrast.} Assume $\tau$ is exchangeable within factor
families: value factors share a true level, momentum factors share a different one. Family
means then absorb $\tau$, and within-family deviations are attributable to $\delta_b$.
This is the workhorse assumption, and it is testable against the factor-zoo literature.
\item \textbf{Projection.} Score a table by its projection onto each candidate signature
after removing family means. Under the null that $\tau$ is exchangeable within families,
that projection has a computable reference distribution, so the output is a $p$-value
rather than a label.
\end{enumerate}

Fitting a classifier from tables to defect labels is the obvious alternative and it does
not survive the sample size. A twelve-year out-of-sample window gives six non-overlapping
two-year observations; three conditions give eighteen labelled examples for a
twenty-two dimensional feature vector, and cross-validation over overlapping windows would
conceal the overfitting rather than reveal it. The signature is measured once from a
pipeline we control and then held fixed. There is nothing to learn, only something to
estimate and something to test.

\subsection{The validation protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

The framework commits in advance to four measurements, in order. Each is a gate: failing
one ends the method rather than downgrading its claims.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Signature stability.} $\delta_b$ must be a property of the defect rather
than of the sample it was measured on. Re-measure on disjoint subperiods and random
half-universes. If \fac{amihud\_illiquidity} does not move sharply positive under universe
conditioning in every subsample, there is no signature.
\item \textbf{Signature uncertainty.} A point estimate cannot support a test. Bootstrap
over dates for a sampling covariance, and report each signature with error bars.
\item \textbf{Separability under realistic noise.} Simulate tables from priors over
$\tau$, apply each defect, add noise at the level a twelve-year study actually carries,
and report the confusion matrix. Whether two defects remain separable at that noise level
is an empirical question with a measurable answer.
\item \textbf{Calibrated application.} Only then run the diagnostic on reported tables,
and report a $p$-value or a posterior over $\{$none, dating, universe, both$\}$, never a
bare label.
\end{enumerate}

Gate three is where labelled data would normally be the binding constraint, and here it is
free. Biased panels are produced by the generator itself, so the confusion matrix can be
estimated over as many synthetic studies as compute allows, with no annotation and no
external dataset. That property is what makes the protocol executable by one researcher,
and it is a reason to prefer this formulation over a supervised one independent of sample
size.

\section{The generator, instantiated}
\label{sec:data}

\paragraph{Universe.} S\&P 500 point-in-time membership, reconstructed as dated
constituent spells from the revision history of a public reference page. This is a free
approximation to a point-in-time constituent file and is not CRSP;
Section~\ref{sec:limits} treats the consequences.

\paragraph{Prices.} Daily adjusted closes, 2010--2026, with a tradability screen on
price, volume and index membership.

\paragraph{Fundamentals.} SEC XBRL company facts, keyed on the date each figure was
filed. Restatements resolve to the earliest filing. Cumulative cash-flow spans are
differenced to true quarters. Rows whose filing date is an XBRL-adoption artifact,
meaning a period ending long before the filing that first reports it, are flagged rather
than silently trusted.

\paragraph{Factors.} Twenty-two published cross-sectional factors, each signed to its
published direction, sector-neutralised and rank-normalised. Eleven read a filed
fundamental. Eleven use only price and volume.

\paragraph{Evaluation.} Information coefficient against 21-day forward returns,
out-of-sample from 2014-01-01, with a purged and embargoed walk-forward following
L\'opez de Prado \cite{lopezdeprado2018}. The evaluation
code is shared across every arm below, so the standard cannot drift between them.

\section{Signature 1: fundamentals dated on fiscal period end}
\label{sec:dating}

A quarter ending 31 March is not public on 31 March. It becomes public when the 10-Q is
filed, a median of 34 days later. Joining on period end hands the backtest a month of
information nobody had.

The generator produces three arms, holding everything else fixed:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{PIT} --- visible the day the filing landed, and correct.
\item \textbf{LAG45} --- every company files 45 days after quarter end, which is common
practice.
\item \textbf{NAIVE} --- visible the instant the quarter closed, which is the defect.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The controls hold exactly}

Eleven factors read no filed figure. Across all three arms their IC is identical, with
maximum absolute drift $\num{0.0e+00}$. That is exact equality in floating point, not a
small number.

This is what licenses the rest of the section. It establishes that nothing in the
generator responds to the filing calendar except the factors that must, and it is the
empirical basis for the exclusion restriction of Section~\ref{sec:framework}.

One factor required care. \fac{turnover\_1m} is built from price and volume but divides
by shares outstanding, so it inherits the filing calendar through its denominator. It is
the only price-family factor that does. We determine dating sensitivity by measurement
and check it against a declared set, so a factor that quietly acquires a filed input is
surfaced rather than hidden among the controls.

The four manufactured significances are \fac{earnings\_yield}, \fac{cash\_flow\_yield},

The comparison between the two rows of Table~\ref{tab:dating} is the practical result. A
fixed 45-day lag is wrong, being right on average and wrong for every filing that arrives
later than that, but it manufactures no false discoveries in this sample. The period-end
join manufactures four. The distinction between ``approximately right'' and ``wrong'' is
the distinction between a bias and a bug, and only the second leaves a signature worth
testing for.

\section{Signature 2: a universe conditioned on current membership}
\label{sec:surv}

Download today's constituents, pull their price history, backtest. The resulting panel
conditions on index membership as of the end of the sample.

\subsection{The dominant channel is not the one usually named}

Survivorship is normally described as dropping losers: the acquired, the bankrupt, the
demoted. In index research that is the smaller half.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Cohort & Names/day & Median \$ volume & Median market cap \\
\midrule
Genuine members & 386 & \$95M & \$10.1B \\
Wrongly \textbf{included}, not yet admitted & \textbf{203} & \textbf{\$28M} & \textbf{\$3.5B} \\
Wrongly \textbf{excluded}, later deleted & 88 & \$54M & \$5.3B \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Daily averages, 2010--2015. The biased panel wrongly includes more than twice as
many names as it wrongly excludes, and the added names are roughly a third the size and a
third the liquidity of a genuine constituent.}
\label{tab:cohorts}
\end{table}

These are companies that were small early in the sample and grew enough to be admitted
later. Conditioning on current membership is therefore not principally a survival filter.
It is a forward-looking growth filter aimed at the small and illiquid cohort, and that
mechanism predicts the shape of the signature before the signature is measured.

\subsection{The distortion relocates rather than inflates}

Mean IC change across all twenty-two factors is $-0.00432$, and only 8 of 22 improve.
Two factors cross $|t| = 2$.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering

\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}

\midrule

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

Illiquidity flips sign and becomes significant; turnover becomes significant with the
wrong sign; the low-volatility anomaly inverts.}
\label{tab:surv}

In a panel conditioned on current membership, being illiquid predicts returns, because the
illiquid names in today's index are precisely those that were small years ago and then
grew into it. Such a panel says: buy the illiquid, high-turnover, high-volatility names.
That is not a risk premium. It is a description of a company on its way into the index,
read backwards.

A defect that lowers mean IC is worth dwelling on, because it is invisible to the summary
statistic an outside reader is most likely to be shown. Nothing about this study's average
performance looks improved. Two of its factors are nonetheless significant for reasons
that have nothing to do with returns.

\label{sec:fingerprints}

The two signatures do not overlap. Figure~\ref{fig:fingerprints} shows both shift vectors
on a common axis, and Table~\ref{tab:families} summarises them by factor family.

Quantitatively, the two vectors correlate at $-0.088$ across the twenty-two factors. They
are not two versions of ``results look better''; they are close to independent directions
in factor space. They also disagree in sign on value: dating inflates
\fac{earnings\_yield} and \fac{roe} by $+0.98$ and $+1.07$ $t$-units, universe
conditioning deflates them by $-1.28$ and $-1.24$.

One property is a hard constraint rather than a tendency. Under dating bias the eleven
price-only factors are unchanged to machine precision, because they never read a filed
figure. An anomalously strong illiquidity result therefore cannot be produced by dating
bias, whatever else is true of the study. This is the exclusion restriction of
Section~\ref{sec:framework} in its strongest available form, and it does most of the
discriminating work without any assumption on $\tau$.

Read forward, the two signatures give the diagnostic its content. A study with a
suspiciously strong illiquidity premium and a dead low-volatility anomaly has a universe
problem. A study whose value and quality factors clear $t = 2$ while its price factors
look ordinary has a dating problem. Section~\ref{sec:protocol} states what has to be
measured before either reading may be given a confidence.

\section{Running the protocol}
\label{sec:gates}

Section~\ref{sec:protocol} commits to four gates. Gates one to three are run here.
Each is computed from a cached per-date IC series for the three panels, which makes
subperiod splits slices of that series and the bootstrap arithmetic rather than a
sequence of panel rebuilds.

\subsection{Gate 1: the signatures are stable}

Both signatures were re-measured on four disjoint subsamples: the first and second
halves of the window, and odd- and even-numbered months. Every subsample signature
correlates with the full-sample vector between $+0.928$ and $+0.995$. The stated
criterion was that \fac{amihud\_illiquidity} must move sharply positive under
universe conditioning in every subsample; it moves $+16.27$, $+9.40$, $+13.65$ and
$+11.86$ $t$-units respectively.

The exclusion restriction was re-checked per subsample rather than only in
aggregate. The maximum absolute shift on the eleven price-only factors under the
dating defect is $\num{0.0e+00}$ in each of the four, so exact invariance is a
property of the construction and not of the full-sample average. Gate 1 passes.

\subsection{Gate 2: the signatures carry error bars}

Two thousand bootstrap draws over dates give a sampling covariance for each
signature. The largest shifts are estimated precisely: under the dating defect
\fac{accruals} shifts $+8.10 \pm 0.41$ and \fac{roe} $+4.92 \pm 0.21$; under
universe conditioning \fac{amihud\_illiquidity} shifts $+17.92 \pm 0.56$ and
\fac{turnover\_1m} $-9.39 \pm 0.33$. The $-0.088$ correlation between the two
signatures has a bootstrap 95\% interval of $[-0.126, -0.053]$, so near-orthogonality
survives estimation rather than being an artefact of a point estimate. Gate 2 passes.

\subsection{Gate 3: one signature is identified and one is not}

Gate three simulates studies, applies a defect, adds sampling noise at the level
this twelve-year window actually carries, and classifies by projection onto each
candidate signature after removing family means. Labels are free because the
generator produces them, so the confusion matrix is estimated over four thousand
simulated studies per condition.

The result depends entirely on one assumption, which is the point of running it.
Under $\tau$ exchangeable within families -- the assumption the inference model of
Section~\ref{sec:framework} is built on -- classification is near-perfect at
99.8\% accuracy. Under a $\tau$ resembling the measured factor table, it splits:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
Diagnostic & Accuracy & Worst class \\
\midrule
None vs.\ universe conditioning & 100.0\% & 100.0\% \\
None vs.\ period-end dating & 52.1\% & 1.6\% \\
Joint, four conditions & 50.8\% & 1.4\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Gate 3 under a realistic $\tau$. The worst-class column is the criterion,
because an average hides the failure that matters: calling a clean table defective.}
\label{tab:gate3}
\end{table}

The universe diagnostic recovers both classes perfectly. The dating diagnostic
labels 97\% of clean tables as defective and is unusable.

The cause is measurable rather than inferred. Write $\tilde{x}$ for a vector with
family means removed. Then $\lVert\tilde{\tau}\rVert = 10.02$,
$\lVert\tilde{\delta}_\text{dating}\rVert = 6.42$ and
$\lVert\tilde{\delta}_\text{universe}\rVert = 23.69$. The dating signature is
\emph{smaller} than the within-family variation of true performance it must be
separated from, and worse, it points the same way:
$\operatorname{corr}(\tilde{\tau}, \tilde{\delta}_\text{dating}) = +0.543$, and the
projection of $\tilde{\tau}$ onto the dating signature is $+0.85$ of it. A clean
table already supplies six-sevenths of the defect's shape for free. The universe
signature has the opposite geometry: correlation $-0.268$ with $\tilde{\tau}$ at
more than twice its magnitude.

A second, structural obstacle compounds it. The exclusion restriction was supposed
to do the discriminating work without assumptions on $\tau$, but the family
partition is nested inside the sensitivity partition. Value, quality and investment
factors are entirely filing-sensitive; momentum, reversal and volatility are
entirely price-only. Only the liquidity family spans both blocks. Within-family
contrasts therefore give the excluded block almost no leverage on $\tau$ in the
block where it is needed, and the strongest identification argument in the
framework is unavailable to the test built on it.

\subsection{What gate 3 establishes}

The framework survives; one of its two instantiations does not. Read as a general
condition, a signature is usable as a diagnostic when two things hold, and both are
measurable before any table is diagnosed:

\begin{enumerate}
\item $\tilde{\delta}_b$ is large relative to $\lVert\tilde{\tau}\rVert$, and not
positively aligned with it. Collinearity between a defect's shape and the true
cross-section of factor performance is not a noise problem and does not improve
with a longer sample.
\item The excluded block spans factor families, so that within-family contrasts
transmit information about $\tau$ from factors the defect cannot touch to factors
it can.
\end{enumerate}

Universe conditioning satisfies both. Period-end dating fails both. That is a
sharper statement than the framework had before the gates were run, and it is
checkable for any future defect without collecting a single reported table.

\section{Scope conditions}
\label{sec:limits}

The framework transfers to any setting where a correct pipeline can be re-run under a
defective convention. The two signatures calibrated here transfer less far, and the
following conditions bound them.

\begin{itemize}
\item One index, one country, 2010--2026. The signatures are measured on the S\&P 500 and
should not be assumed to hold for small caps or non-US markets. Re-measurement is a run of
the generator, not a new study.
\item Membership spells are reconstructed from a public wiki revision history, not CRSP.
The universe signature inherits whatever that approximation gets wrong.
\item Fundamental coverage begins in 2010 because of the XBRL mandate phase-in, so the
dating signature says nothing about the pre-XBRL era.
\item The two arms are not additively decomposable. The universe arm rebuilds forward
returns per universe, because a study with that defect never observes the returns of the
names it excludes. Real studies contain several defects at once, and whether signatures
compose is a question for gate three.
\item The diagnostic needs a wide enough table to work with. A study reporting five
factors gives a five-dimensional observation, and the exclusion restriction does not apply
at all if none of the five are price-only.
\item Gates one through three are run in Section~\ref{sec:gates}. Gate four,
calibrated application to reported tables, is not, and applies only to the universe
signature: the dating signature does not survive gate three.
\item The gate 3 simulation draws $\tau$ around this study's own measured factor
table. That is the most realistic $\tau$ available, and it is one draw from one
market and one window. A different factor cross-section could have a different
alignment with the dating signature, which is an argument for measuring the
alignment per application rather than assuming this one transfers.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

Data-handling defects have shapes. A period-end join inflates fundamental factors
uniformly by 59\% of true IC and manufactures four false discoveries while leaving price
factors provably untouched. Conditioning a universe on current membership relocates rather
than inflates, flipping illiquidity to significance and inverting the low-volatility
anomaly, through a channel better described as a forward-looking growth filter than as a
survival filter. Neither shape is visible in a Sharpe ratio, and both are visible in the
cross-section of what a study reports.

Bias fingerprinting takes that observation and builds a method on it: measure the shape
once on a pipeline you own, then test for it in tables you do not. The pieces the method
needs are in place. A generator that isolates one convention at a time, two signatures
that are near-orthogonal and carry an exact exclusion restriction, an inference model that
absorbs unknown true performance through family contrasts, and a protocol whose hardest
step has free labelled data because the labels are generated rather than collected.

Running the protocol splits that claim in two. Both signatures are stable and both
carry tight error bars, but only universe conditioning is separable from clean
performance at the noise level of a twelve-year study. Period-end dating is not, and
the reason generalises: its shape is nearly parallel to the true cross-section of
factor performance, so a correct table already looks like a defective one. That is a
property of the defect, not a shortage of data, and no longer window fixes it.

What the framework gains from failing that gate is a condition rather than a caveat.
A signature is diagnostically usable when it is large relative to within-family
variation in true performance, not positively aligned with it, and its excluded
block spans factor families. All three are measurable on a pipeline the analyst
already owns, before any reported table is touched. The rest of the catalogue --
delisting-return assumptions, restatement handling, index-reconstitution timing --
can therefore be screened for usability at the point of calibration, and the ones
that fail can be reported as measurements of what a defect costs without being
promoted to diagnostics.
